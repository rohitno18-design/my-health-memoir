# I M Smrti — Distribution

How the content actually reaches people. 29 August 2026.

---

## The thesis

> **Stop trying to get seen. Start trying to get sent.**

Three separate facts landed on the same point:

1. **Instagram's algorithm** — Mosseri has confirmed the top signals as watch time, **sends per reach**, and likes per reach. Sends carry roughly **3–5× the weight of likes** for reaching people who don't follow you. Over 70% of feed content is now algorithm-recommended, so a small account can reach far — but only via sends.
2. **Indian health information already travels by forward.** Family WhatsApp groups are where health content actually circulates in India — researchers call them "meso-news spaces", part conversation, part news, and it's where the misinformation lives too.
3. **Your content is inherently sendable.** Caregiver content has a specific recipient built in. Nobody sends a motivational quote to their father. They absolutely send "Papa, dekho."

**So the unit of distribution is not a follower. It's a forward.**

---

## 1. THE INSIGHT WORTH BUILDING ON

Research on Indian family WhatsApp groups found something specific: young Indians believe their parents fall for health misinformation, **but correcting them directly is socially difficult and rarely happens.** Arguing with your father about his health is a fight nobody wants.

**Forwarding a video is not a fight.**

That is the gap this page fills. You are the third party that lets a 32-year-old correct their 60-year-old parent **without it being a confrontation.** They don't have to say "you're wrong." They send a link and let you say it.

### What this means for the content
Every post should be **something one person can send to another person instead of having an argument.**

| Weak (nobody forwards this) | Strong (a specific person forwards this to a specific person) |
|---|---|
| "5 tips for better health" | "Send this to your father if he stopped his BP tablet because he 'feels fine' now" |
| "Understanding cholesterol" | "For the uncle who says ghee is the problem" |
| "Vitamin D awareness" | "For your mother, who's been tired for two years and calls it age" |

**The rule:** before writing any post, name the person receiving it and the person sending it. If you can't, the post has no distribution.

---

## 2. DESIGN FOR THE SEND

### The send line
Every post gets one explicit line naming a recipient. Not "share this" — **"send this to ___."**

- *"Send this to whoever picks up the medicines in your house."*
- *"Send this to the person who says 'sab theek hai' every time you ask."*
- *"Send this to your sibling who thinks you're overreacting about Papa's reports."*

This is *not* a CTA in the promotional sense, so it doesn't violate the 50%-no-CTA rule. It's a **routing instruction** — it costs the viewer nothing and asks nothing for you.

### Make it forwardable off-platform too
Health content in India dies at the Instagram boundary unless you help it cross:
- **Burn a readable title into the first frame** so it makes sense as a screenshot
- **Carousels double as WhatsApp images** — one clear point per slide, legible at thumbnail size
- Keep reels **under 60s** so they can be downloaded and forwarded whole
- Never rely on the caption to carry meaning — captions don't survive a forward

### Two-way conversation is now a ranked signal
As of March 2026, silence in comments and DMs costs reach. **Replying is distribution, not admin.** Reply to every comment in the first hour — that first-hour engagement is what triggers the Explore test that decides the post's ceiling.

## 3. THE DOCTOR PARTNERSHIP — the highest-leverage move available

This is the one to act on first, because **one person solves four problems.**

### Why doctors need you
The NMC Professional Conduct Regulations (2023) bar registered doctors from self-promotion, soliciting patients, endorsing products, posting patient reports or scans, and patient testimonials. But NMC explicitly *endorses* social media for **public health discussion, myth-busting, and tackling medical misinformation.**

So a doctor cannot build an audience by promoting themselves — **but they can appear in educational, non-promotional content.** That's exactly what this page is. You are one of the few formats a compliance-conscious doctor can safely join.

### Why you need them
| Problem | Solved by a doctor partner |
|---|---|
| ASCI qualification requirement | Content is medically reviewed |
| 66 reference ranges unvalidated | Clinical sign-off *(also fixes the app)* |
| No credibility on a new page | "Medically reviewed by Dr. X" |
| No distribution | Collab posts reach both audiences |

**One person. Four problems. This is the single highest-value action in the entire content plan.**

### How to run it
- **Target:** a physician, endocrinologist, or pathologist with 5k–50k followers who already posts educational content. Pathologists are the sharpest fit — they read reports all day and almost none of them have an audience.
- **Offer:** a named medical-reviewer credit, co-authored content, and reach — not a fee, at least initially.
- **Format:** Instagram **Collab posts** (co-authored, appears on both grids, engagement pools combine). Reported at roughly **2.2× the reach** of a solo post — vendor data, so treat as directional, but the mechanic is real and Instagram doesn't favour either account.
- **Cadence:** 1–2 per month. More reads as spam.
- **Sizing:** for an account under 5k, partner with accounts **2–3× your size.** Adjacent niche, not identical.

### The format to build together
**"Doctor reacts to a real report."** You bring the anonymised report and the family's question; they bring the clinical read. You can show the report *(they legally can't on their own)*; they can interpret it *(you legally shouldn't alone)*. **Neither of you could make this content without the other.** That's a moat.

## 4. THE DM ENGINE

Treat DMs as the product, not the overflow.

Every stranger who sends a report question is simultaneously:
- a future app user
- a free content idea, in their own words
- a data point for the ground-truth set the data layer is missing
- a send, which is the strongest reach signal there is

**Rules:**
- Reply to every single one until 500 followers. It doesn't scale — that's exactly why it works now.
- Never interpret their specific report. Reply with: *what that test measures, what question to ask their doctor, and the disclaimer.* Helpful, and inside the compliance line.
- Log every question in the story bank. **The questions you're asked are the content calendar.**
- When someone says "this helped" — ask permission to share it. That's how the missing user-story bank gets built, one at a time, from day one.

---

## 5. COMMENTS AS A CHANNEL

For a cold account, a genuinely useful comment on a large health post reaches more people than your own post does.

**The rule: be useful, never promotional.** Never mention the app. Never say "check my page." Add the thing the original post left out — a reference range, what the abbreviation means, what to ask the doctor. Curiosity brings people to the profile; the profile does the selling.

**Where:** doctor-creators, pathology labs, diabetes and thyroid communities, and the comments under any large Indian health post where people are visibly confused.

**Budget:** 15 minutes a day, 5–10 comments. Zero cost, and it compounds.

---

## 6. THE FIRST 100 — seeding

Cold accounts don't get algorithmic reach until humans engage first. Manufacture that once, honestly:

1. **Your own network.** You're already the person friends and family call about health. Send week 1's posts directly to the twenty people who have actually asked you a health question. Not "please follow" — *"I made this, it answers what you asked me last month."*
2. **Family WhatsApp groups.** The most natural distribution you have, and the exact channel this content is designed for.
3. **Ask for the send, not the follow.** "If it's useful, send it to one person" outperforms "please follow" and builds the signal that actually drives reach.

Do **not** buy followers, use engagement pods, or buy shares. Pods are detected and get accounts removed from recommendations entirely — and on a health page tied to a real product, it's not a risk worth taking for a vanity number.

## 7. CROSS-PLATFORM — one shoot, four places

Every reel is already made. Posting it elsewhere costs minutes.

| Platform | Why | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **YouTube Shorts** | Hindi report-explainer content already exists there with demand, and Shorts have a long tail Instagram doesn't. Search traffic compounds. | Upload, retitle with a searchable question |
| **WhatsApp Channel** | Where Indian health information actually lives. Zero algorithm — everyone sees it. | Post the same video |
| **Instagram** | Home base | — |
| **YouTube long-form** *(month 4+)* | "Read a full report with me" — 10 min. Highest-trust format, and it's where searches like "HbA1c kya hota hai" land | Later. Don't start here. |

**Rules:** upload natively everywhere — never post a watermarked download. Reposts and watermarked content get 40–60% less distribution and can trigger an aggregator penalty.

---

## 8. THE 90-DAY PLAN

### Days 1–30 — prove the content
- Post the 16 scripted posts
- Reply to every comment within the first hour
- 5 comments/day on larger health accounts
- Seed each post to your own network and family groups
- **Find and sign the doctor reviewer** ← start day 1, it's the long-lead item
- *Measure:* saves and sends per post. Nothing else.

### Days 31–60 — prove distribution
- First **2 Collab posts** with the doctor partner
- Launch "Doctor reacts to a real report" as a repeating format
- Start the WhatsApp Channel, mirror to YouTube Shorts
- Turn the top 5 DM questions into posts, credited to the asker *(with permission)*
- *Measure:* does collab reach beat solo reach? Are DMs growing?

### Days 61–90 — compound
- 2 collabs/month, now with a second partner
- First real user stories, if consent allows
- Double down on whichever pillar produced the most **sends** — not the most likes
- *Measure:* strangers DMing photos of their reports. **That's the thesis proving itself.**

---

## 9. WHAT NOT TO DO

- ❌ Buy followers, likes or shares — low-quality signal, account-wide reach penalty
- ❌ Engagement pods — detected, removes you from recommendations
- ❌ Repost watermarked video from another platform — aggregator penalty
- ❌ More than 2 collabs/month — reads as spam
- ❌ Collab with an identical account — dilutes rather than expands; go adjacent
- ❌ Chase follower count — it is the one metric with no relationship to installs

---

## The one number

If you track a single thing for 90 days, track **sends per post.**

It's the strongest reach signal Instagram has, it's the behaviour that carries content into the WhatsApp groups where Indian health information actually spreads, and it's the truest measure of whether a person thought of a *specific human being* while watching.

That last part is the whole business.
