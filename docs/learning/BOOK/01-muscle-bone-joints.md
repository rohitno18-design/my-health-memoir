# Part 1 — Muscle, Bone & Joints

---

## 1.1 — Muscle doesn't grow in the gym. It grows in recovery.
*Taught 2026-09-22*

Training doesn't build muscle. Training creates the *demand* for muscle.

When you lift, you create thousands of microscopic tears in the muscle fibre —
actual mechanical damage. That's the stimulus. The growth happens afterwards,
when the body repairs those fibres slightly thicker than they were, using
protein as the raw material and sleep as the main window in which the repair
work actually gets done.

This is why two people can do the identical workout and get completely
different results. The workout was the same. The other 23 hours weren't.

Two mechanisms sit underneath this:

**Progressive overload.** A muscle only has a reason to grow if the demand on
it keeps increasing — more weight, more reps, or better control of the same
weight. The body is efficient and slightly lazy: it will not build tissue it
doesn't need. This is why plateaus happen. A plateau is almost never a
genetics problem, it's a demand problem.

**DOMS (delayed-onset muscle soreness).** The soreness that arrives a day or
two after a new movement is the repair process running, not damage you should
worry about. But soreness is not the goal and it's a poor measure of a good
session — you can build muscle perfectly well without being sore, and you can
be very sore from a session that built almost nothing.

**Your body:** You've lived progressive overload for 22 years without
necessarily naming it. The question worth asking: where in your current
routine is something still genuinely progressing — weight, reps, or control —
and where has it quietly gone flat and stayed flat?

**Dad's body:** Same principle, different entry point. At 60, starting from
zero, tissue repairs slower and joints already carry decades of wear. The real
risk in week one is not "lifting too little" — it's reaching for weight before
movement pattern and control are earned. That's not a watered-down version of
training. For where he's starting, it's the correct version.
