# I M Smrti — Week 1 Scripts (Posts 1–4)

Production-ready. Shoot all four in one sitting.

**Standing rules applied to every post below:**
- Ends on the handoff — about *them*, not about you
- No diagnosis, no dose recommendation, no "stop your medicine"
- Language: simple Hinglish, English for anything printed on a report

> ### ⚠️ DISCLAIMER GOES FIRST, NOT LAST
> ASCI Addendum II (April 2025) requires an unqualified health creator's
> disclaimer to appear **upfront** — superimposed at the start of a video or as
> the opening remark, not buried at the end.
>
> **On every reel:** on-screen at 0:00, small, top of frame, held 2 seconds:
> `Not a doctor. Health information, not medical advice.`
>
> **On every carousel:** same line on slide 1, small, under the hook.
>
> **On every caption:** same line as the **first** line.
>
> Keep the closing "show this to your doctor" too — that's the handoff, and it
> does a different job. The upfront line is the compliance one.

# POST 1 — CAROUSEL
## "Your prescription is written in code"

**Goal:** Prove utility on day one. This is the highest-save item in the whole bank — someone will screenshot it before their next appointment. No CTA except *save*.

**Format:** 8 slides, 4:5 portrait
**Cover style:** text-led, no face

---

### Slide 1 — Cover
> # Your prescription is written in code.
> ### Here's the key.
>
> *Save this before your next doctor visit →*

---

### Slide 2
> ## 1-0-1
> ### The most common one — and the most misread.
>
> Three positions = **morning – afternoon – night**
>
> **1-0-1** → one tablet morning, nothing afternoon, one at night
> **0-0-1** → only at night
> **1-1-1** → three times a day
> **½-0-½** → half tablet morning, half at night

---

### Slide 3
> ## OD · BD · TDS · QID
> ### Latin. Still used. Every single day.
>
> **OD** → once a day
> **BD** → twice a day
> **TDS** *(or TID)* → three times a day
> **QID** *(or QDS)* → four times a day
>
> *If you see BD, that's every 12 hours — not "twice whenever".*

---

### Slide 4
> ## HS · SOS · STAT
> ### The three that cause the most confusion.
>
> **HS** → at bedtime
> **SOS** → **only if needed.** It does *not* mean emergency.
> **STAT** → immediately, right now
>
> *SOS is the one people get wrong. If your painkiller says SOS, it is not a daily tablet.*

---

### Slide 5
> ## Before food or after?
>
> **AC** → before food
> **PC** → after food
>
> And "khaane ke baad" means roughly **30 minutes after** you finish —
> not with the last bite of roti.
>
> *Some medicines genuinely don't work if this is wrong.*

---

### Slide 6
> ## Why this isn't a small thing
>
> A bedtime tablet taken at breakfast.
> An "only if needed" painkiller taken every day for a month.
> An antibiotic stopped on day 3 because you felt better.
>
> **Same medicine. Completely different effect on your body.**

---

### Slide 7
> ## Ask this before you leave the room
>
> ### "Doctor, kitni baar — aur khaane se pehle ya baad?"
>
> Ten seconds in the clinic.
> Saves a week of guessing at home.

---

### Slide 8 — Close
> ## Save this.
> ### Send it to whoever manages your parents' medicines.
>
> ---
> *Not medical advice. Always follow what your doctor wrote.*
> **@imsmrti**

---

### Caption
```
Your prescription is written in code. Here's the key.

1-0-1 means morning – afternoon – night. So 0-0-1 is a
night-only tablet, and 1-1-1 is three times a day.

The one people get wrong most often is SOS. It does not
mean emergency — it means ONLY IF NEEDED. If your
painkiller says SOS, it was never meant to be daily.

And "khaane ke baad" means about 30 minutes after you
finish eating, not with the last bite.

If you're the person in your family who picks up the
medicines, save this one.

Not medical advice. Always follow what your doctor wrote.
```

# POST 2 — REEL (~45s)
## "Boiled food is not health food"

**Goal:** Plant the flag. This is the post that tells people what this page believes. Expect argument in the comments — that's the point. **No CTA.**

**Shot:** You to camera. Kitchen or plain wall. One continuous take if possible — the conviction matters more than the edit.

---

### Script

> **[0:00 — hard open, no greeting]**
> **"Boiled food is not health food."**
>
> **[0:03]**
> Every Indian health page tells you the same thing.
> Stop the ghee. Stop the spices. Eat boiled vegetables. Eat salad.
>
> **[0:10]**
> And you try it. For four days.
> Then you quit — and you feel like *you* failed.
>
> **[0:15]**
> You didn't fail. **The advice failed.**
>
> **[0:18]**
> Because that advice is copied. From countries that don't cook the way we cook.
> We've been cooking properly for three thousand years. We're not switching to boiled food now — and we shouldn't have to.
>
> **[0:27]**
> Here's the actual problem.
> It was never the spices. It was never the ghee.
>
> **[0:32]**
> It's the packet. It's the portion. It's the four o'clock biscuit.
>
> **[0:36]**
> I removed sugar, alcohol and fast food.
> I removed **nothing** from my kitchen.
>
> **[0:41]**
> **Taste is not the enemy. Junk is.**
>
> **[0:44]**
> Cook your food. Eat your food. Just stop eating the packet.

---

**On-screen text (large, 3 cards only — don't caption every word):**
- `BOILED FOOD IS NOT HEALTH FOOD`
- `You didn't fail. The advice failed.`
- `Taste is not the enemy. Junk is.`

**B-roll if you want cutaways:** tadka in a pan, spice box, a packet of biscuits (shot unflatteringly), home-cooked thali.

### Caption
```
Boiled food is not health food.

Every health page in India gives the same advice — no ghee,
no spice, boiled vegetables. You last four days, you quit,
and you decide you have no discipline.

You have plenty of discipline. The advice was wrong for you.

It was copied from countries that don't cook the way we do.
We've been cooking well for three thousand years. The problem
was never the tadka.

The problem is the packet, the portion, and the 4pm biscuit.

I cut sugar, alcohol and fast food. I cut nothing from my
kitchen.

Taste is not the enemy. Junk is.

Not medical advice.
```

# POST 3 — REEL (~55s)
## "This report said 8.4. Normal starts at 30."

**Goal:** Prove you can read a report. This is the competence post. **No CTA** — let the utility speak.

**Shot:** Open on the actual report (blurred name), then cut to you. Or full voiceover over the report — either works.

---

### Script

> **[0:00 — over the report, finger on the number]**
> **"This report said 8.4. Normal starts at 30."**
>
> **[0:05]**
> That's not slightly low. That's **severe deficiency**.
> And the person holding this report had no idea — because nobody explained it to them.
>
> **[0:12]**
> So let me explain it.
>
> **[0:14]**
> Vitamin D isn't just a bone vitamin. It's how your body absorbs calcium, keeps your muscles working, and keeps you from feeling exhausted for no reason.
>
> **[0:22]**
> When it's low, it feels like:
> tiredness that sleep doesn't fix. Body ache. Bone or joint pain. Weak muscles. Low mood.
>
> **[0:31]**
> Sounds like normal life in India, doesn't it?
> **That's the problem. It's so common we stopped noticing it.**
>
> **[0:36 — cite the number, this is what makes it factual not advisory]**
> Metropolis looked at **22 lakh** Vitamin D tests in India.
> **46% were deficient. Another 26% were insufficient.**
> That's roughly **7 out of 10** people who got tested.
>
> **[0:44]**
> Why here specifically — we stay indoors, we cover up, pollution blocks the sunlight, and there's very little of it in a vegetarian diet.
>
> **[0:45]**
> Now — do not self-medicate on this.
> Those 60,000 IU sachets are **weekly**, not daily, and there's a reason for that. Get tested, then ask your doctor.
>
> **[0:53]**
> But do get tested.
> If you're tired all the time and you don't know why — this is one blood test that might explain years of it.

---

**On-screen text:**
- `8.4` *(huge)* → then `NORMAL STARTS AT 30`
- `Tired · Body ache · Weak muscles · Low mood`
- `So common we stopped noticing`
- `Get tested. Then ask your doctor.`

**⚠️ Before you post:** blur the patient name, lab name, and any ID on the report. If it's a family member's, get their permission.

### Caption
```
8.4 — when normal starts at 30.

That's severe Vitamin D deficiency, and the person holding
this report didn't know, because nobody explained it.

Low Vitamin D feels like: tiredness that sleep doesn't fix,
body ache, bone or joint pain, weak muscles, low mood.

Read that list again. It sounds like normal life for half
the people you know. That's exactly the problem — it's so
common in India that we stopped calling it a problem.

Why us: indoors all day, covered up, pollution cutting the
sunlight, and very little of it in the average Indian diet.

Please don't self-medicate. Those 60,000 IU sachets are
weekly, not daily, and your dose isn't for the internet to
decide. Get tested, then take the report to your doctor.

But get tested. One blood test might explain years of
feeling tired.

Not medical advice. Always show your reports to your doctor.
```
